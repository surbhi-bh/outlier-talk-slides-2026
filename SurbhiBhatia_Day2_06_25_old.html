Good AI prompt design requires human judgement.
claude · genre-prompt · v1 — pick relevant genres v1 / 4
Prompt v1: Pick relevant genres For each Bollywood movie in my input [year, movie], pick relevant genres from this list: "Action/Adventure", "Drama", "Romance", "Comedy", "Thriller/Crime", "Fantasy/Sci-Fi/Horror", "Social Issues", "Biopic", "Propaganda", "Period/Historical", "Family Saga", "Sports", "Musical", "Coming-of-Age", "Political", "War"… Result: too many labels. Every movie fits 3 of them.▍
claude · genre-prompt · v2 — + Masala v2 / 4
Prompt v2: + Masala "Masala": A Masala film is a mainstream Indian movie that blends multiple genres (typically action, romance, comedy, drama, and music) in a single storyline. These films often include stylized action, melodrama, emotional plot twists, and frequent song-and-dance sequences. The tone is usually exaggerated, aiming for mass appeal and entertainment. — same six categories as v1 — Result: the model labelled half the dataset Masala. Useless.▍
claude · genre-prompt · v3 — + Nota v3 / 4
Prompt v3: + Nota "Masala": …blends multiple genres in a single storyline… "Nota": None of the above. Films that don't clearly fit into any of the six categories — use sparingly. — same six categories as v1 — Result: model treats Nota as a comfortable escape hatch. Anything ambiguous gets dumped here. The dataset loses signal.▍
claude · genre-prompt · v4 — final v4 / 4
Prompt v4 (final): Primary Genre Tagging You are an expert film critic who can determine the primary genre of a Bollywood movie by analyzing its core elements – plot, cast, themes, poster, narrative structure. For each movie in my input [year, movie], I want you to respond with a comma separated file of: [movie, year, justification, primary_genre]. The justification is a short snippet explaining why the movie best fits the chosen genre. primary_genre labels come from these definitions (if the movie fits multiple genres, pick the most suitable one). "Action/Adventure": Films focused on physical challenges, excitement, and spectacle. Features pursuits, fights, stunts, explosions, or daring quests. Heroes often face external threats or obstacles. Emphasis on thrilling sequences, physical fights and big spectacle conflicts. "Drama/Serious": Character-driven stories dealing with realistic emotional conflicts, moral dilemmas, or social issues. Focus on relationships, personal growth, or psychological development. Tone is typically serious and grounded, aiming for emotional depth. "Romance": Stories with a love story at its core. Includes traditional love stories, passionate love. Includes romantic comedies, where romance is enhanced by comedy but humor is a subplot — lighthearted, feel-good stories with uplifting endings; also romantic dramas, which explore deep emotional themes like family, cultural traditions, or personal struggles, adding intensity to the romance. A key test: if removing the romantic storyline would make the film fall apart, it's likely a romance film. "Comedy": Films primarily intended to amuse and entertain through humor, whether subtle or broad. Includes farce, satire — but excludes romantic comedies. Made to make the audience laugh; tone is upbeat and playful. "Thriller/Crime": Stories involving criminal activity, mystery, suspense, or psychological tension. Focus on investigation, pursuit of justice, or moral ambiguity. Often features twists, rising tension, and complex plotting. "Fantasy/Sci-Fi/Horror": Films featuring supernatural, futuristic, or horror elements that depart from everyday reality. Includes magical worlds, advanced technology, alien life, monsters, or psychological horror. "Masala" · "Nota"▍
PROMPT FATIGUE
  • Started prompting.
  • Buckets of genres.
  • But a movie can be a cocktail of genres.
  • In India — that's called a masala film.
  • Tried Masala as a category.
  • Tried NOTA — none of the above.
  • Kept tweaking — till I was fatigued.
  • Finally decided — most dominant genre per film.
  • Based on plot, cast, visual style, narrative.
  • An editorial choice.
